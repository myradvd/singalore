Find the small tower at the heart of the garden. It sits on an eight sided base, and short flights of steps climb up to it from four sides, one for each point of the compass. On top is a Chinese roof in two tiers, one set above the other. This is the Nanyang University Memorial, also known as the Former Nanyang University Memorial. It is modest in size, but it carries one of the great fundraising stories in Singapore's history.

Take a moment to look around before we begin. You are in Yunnan Garden, on the campus of Nanyang Technological University in Jurong West. On the higher ground nearby stands the old library and administration building of Nanyang University, with its own sweeping Chinese roof. The memorial was placed in the garden in front of it, and the two belong to the same story.

That story starts in 1953, with a Hokkien rubber magnate named Tan Lark Sye. He chaired the Singapore Chinese Chamber of Commerce and was president of the Singapore Hokkien Huay Kuan. Early in 1953 he proposed a university for the Chinese community, one that would teach in Chinese.

The plan had several goals. It would give Chinese educated young people a proper path to higher learning. It would protect and pass on Chinese culture and values. And it would train teachers for the high schools of Malaya. The new school would be called Nanyang University, and people soon gave it an affectionate short name, Nantah.

Money and land came next. Tan Lark Sye gave five million dollars of his own money. The Hokkien Huay Kuan gave a huge stretch of land in Jurong, more than five hundred acres, for the campus where you now stand. The association also helped to raise funds.

What makes this place special is who else gave. Donations came from every level of Chinese society. Wealthy merchants gave, but so did hawkers, taxi drivers and washerwomen. Cabaret girls and dance hostesses gave. Trishaw riders are among the donors most often remembered. One woodblock print from the period shows trishaw riders banding together to hand over a whole day's earnings to the university fund.

This memorial was built to honour those donors. Most of them were ordinary working people. The tower is a thank you to all of them at once.

Building work on the campus began in 1954. The architect for the first phase was Ng Keng Siang, one of the first Singapore born architects with a university training. He had studied in London and New York, and he ran one of the most successful practices in the colony. His other work includes the Asia Insurance Building in the city and the Lim Bo Seng Memorial in Esplanade Park.

For Nantah, Ng used a style known as the Chinese National style. Look closely at the memorial and you can see how it works. The body of the structure is modern reinforced concrete. On top sits a roof in a distinctly Chinese form. The idea was to marry the new building methods of the day with a look that spoke of Chinese tradition. The library building and the university's entrance arch were designed in the same style, so the three structures look like members of one family.

While the campus was being built, students did not wait. Preparatory classes were held at two Chinese schools in town, The Chinese High School and Chung Cheng High School. Then, on 15 March 1956, the university flag was raised on the new campus in a simple ceremony, marking the start of classes. The first intake was 584 students, spread across three faculties: arts, science and commerce.

The grand day came two years later. On 30 March 1958, Nanyang University held its official opening. A crowd of around fifty thousand well wishers came out to Jurong to see it. On the same day this memorial was unveiled, marking the end of the first phase of building. The Governor of Singapore, Sir William Goode, performed the unveiling at a ceremony with more than a hundred guests. Some accounts say Tan Lark Sye stood beside him.

The first class of graduates, 437 of them, left the university in 1959. By 1963, enrolment had grown to 2,324 students. In 1980, it was merged by law with the University of Singapore to form the National University of Singapore. The Nantah campus fell quiet. Records differ slightly on dates, but within a year or two a new institution, the Nanyang Technological Institute, moved onto the grounds. It later grew into the university that occupies this campus today, Nanyang Technological University. In 1996, the alumni rolls of the old Nanyang University were transferred to NTU, tying the two institutions together on paper as well as on the ground.

In 1998, the memorial was gazetted as a national monument, together with the old library and administration building and the original entrance arch. The three structures were protected as a single monument. That is the highest level of protection a building can receive in Singapore.

The garden around the memorial has also changed. It was recently reworked as part of a project to renew Yunnan Garden, which linked the plaza in front of the old library, the garden itself and Nanyang Lake into one connected landscape. The memorial was restored during that work.

Before you move on, walk once around the base. Notice how the steps rise from four directions, so the tower can be approached from any side. Look up at the two layers of roof, and at how small the whole structure is beside the trees and lawns. Then think about the crowd that stood out here in 1958, and the trishaw riders who handed over a whole day's earnings so that a campus could rise on this land. This tower was put up to remember them.
